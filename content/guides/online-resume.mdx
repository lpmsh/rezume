Emailing your resume as a PDF attachment is the default — but it's also the worst part of a job search. Files get buried in inboxes, the version you sent goes stale the moment you update it, and you never find out whether anyone actually opened it.

Putting your resume **online as a link** fixes all three problems. Here's how to do it in a few minutes.

## Why a link beats an attachment

A hosted resume link is more than a convenience:

- **It's permanent.** Share one URL on every application and it never breaks, even after you update the file behind it.
- **It's measurable.** You can see when your resume is opened instead of guessing.
- **It's cleaner.** A tidy link looks far more professional than `resume_final_v3.pdf`.

## How to put your resume online

1. **Upload your PDF.** Start with the resume you already have — no reformatting needed.
2. **Claim your slug.** Pick a short, memorable URL like `rezume.so/yourname`.
3. **Share the link.** Drop it into applications, your [LinkedIn profile](/guides/resume-link-for-linkedin), email signatures, and anywhere else.

When you need to make changes, upload a new PDF. The link stays the same, so you never have to re-share it.

## Keep one link forever

The biggest win is that your resume lives at a single, stable address. Update it the night before an interview and every recruiter who already has your link sees the latest version automatically.
