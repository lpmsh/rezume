When you're a student or recent grad, your resume is a moving target. You add an internship, finish a class project, pick up a leadership role — and the version you handed out last month is already behind. Re-sending updated PDFs to everyone is a hassle, and most students don't have a personal website to point people to. A single **resume link** solves both problems: it's free, it needs no website, and you update it once instead of everywhere.

## Why students don't need a website

There's a common assumption that "putting your resume online" means building a site. It doesn't. A hosted resume link gives you the one thing you actually need — a shareable web address for your resume — without a domain to buy, a builder to learn, or anything to maintain between classes. You upload the PDF you already made for class or the career center, claim a short slug, and you're done. (If you want the details, see [hosting your resume online for free](/guides/host-resume-online).)

That matters early in a career, when spending time and money on a website is overkill for a resume that's still taking shape.

## Where students share their resume link

A link fits the places students actually job hunt:

- **Career center portals** — paste your link, or upload your PDF and add the link in a notes field.
- **Career fairs** — print the link on a name tag or generate a QR code so recruiters can grab your resume without carrying paper.
- **[Job applications](/guides/resume-link-for-job-applications)** — add it to the website field, and upload the file where required.
- **[LinkedIn](/guides/resume-link-for-linkedin)** — in your Featured section, especially while you're job searching.
- **Networking** — easy to mention in a message to an alum or a recruiter you met.

## Built for a resume that keeps changing

The single best fit for students is that your resume is never finished, and the link is built for exactly that. Add a new project the night before an interview and every recruiter who already has your link sees it — no re-sending, no "please use this newer version." The URL you create as a sophomore is the one you'll still be using when you graduate and start applying for full-time roles.

## One link now, the same link later

Set up your resume link today, put it on your applications and profiles, and just swap in new versions as your experience grows. It costs nothing, needs no website, and grows with you.
