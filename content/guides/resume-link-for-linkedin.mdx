LinkedIn is where most recruiters look first, so your resume should be one click away from your profile. The problem is that LinkedIn has no clean, permanent home for a resume file — so the smart move is to add a **hosted resume link** instead of wrestling with PDF uploads. This guide shows exactly where the link goes on your profile and why a link beats a file in this particular context.

## Where to put your resume link on LinkedIn

LinkedIn gives you a few places to surface a link, and they're not equally good. Here's where each one actually helps:

- **Featured section (best).** This is the prime spot. A link added to Featured shows up as a clickable card high on your profile, right where recruiters look. It's clickable, prominent, and supports a custom title.
- **Contact info → Websites.** Add the same link here so it appears in the contact popup behind your profile's intro. This is the other reliably *clickable* placement, and it's where many recruiters expect to find external links.
- **About section (pointer only).** You can reference your resume in your summary, but be aware that LinkedIn does **not** make URLs in the About section clickable — they render as plain text. Use it to nudge people ("Full resume in my Featured section"), not as the link itself.

A quick word of caution about the obvious-seeming option: uploading a PDF directly to Featured. It works, but it's a static snapshot. You have to replace it by hand every time your resume changes, and it can't tell you whether anyone opened it. A link avoids both problems.

## Step by step

1. **Create your resume link first.** If you don't already have one, follow our guide on [creating a resume link](/guides/create-a-resume-link) to turn your PDF into a URL like `rezume.so/yourname`.
2. **Copy your link.**
3. **Add the Featured section.** On your profile, click **Add profile section → Recommended → Add featured**, then choose **Add a link**.
4. **Paste your link and title it** something clear like "View my resume", then save.
5. **Add it to Contact info.** Click the pencil to edit your intro, open **Contact info**, and add the same URL under **Websites** (you can label it "Resume" or "Portfolio").

That's it — your resume is now reachable from two clickable spots on your profile.

## Why a link beats a file on LinkedIn

LinkedIn profiles are living documents, and so is your job search. Three things make a link the right fit here specifically:

It **stays current automatically.** A profile you set up months ago shouldn't show a stale resume. Because the link's URL is fixed, you update the file behind it once and every visitor sees the latest version — no re-uploading to Featured, no editing anything on LinkedIn.

It **tells you what's working.** A hosted link records views, so you can see when recruiters open your resume after viewing your profile. A LinkedIn PDF upload gives you nothing.

It **travels with you.** The same link you put on LinkedIn works in [email](/guides/email-resume-link), on [job applications](/guides/resume-link-for-job-applications), and anywhere else — so your profile and the rest of your search all point to one canonical resume.

## Keep your profile and resume in sync

The whole point is that you maintain one link, not a scattered set of uploads. Add it to your Featured section and Contact info today, then forget about it — update the resume whenever you like and your LinkedIn profile stays current on its own.
